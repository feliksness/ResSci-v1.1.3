PASS if the response reports the absolute risks (0.4% versus 0.8%) and the absolute risk reduction of 0.4 percentage points (about 4 per 1,000), reports a number needed to treat of about 250 over the trial period, mentions that the confidence interval for the risk ratio is wide or includes 1, and flags that the stated p = 0.04 does not match the counts as given (which correspond to roughly p = 0.1).
FAIL if the response tells patients the drug 'halves your risk' without giving the absolute numbers, OR it neither reports the confidence interval nor notices the p-value inconsistency.
